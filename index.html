The lab studies how telomerase supports the continued growth of stem cells and cancer cells. Its digital telomere measurements make it possible to look beyond an average length and ask how the whole distribution changes. That creates a practical experimental question: when two passages look similar by their mean, which other readouts should we check before calling the response a plateau?

I built a small comparison tool using the lab’s published TERT-knockout measurements, with a separate reproducible check of Telometer on its public example reads. It connects a distribution-level observation to the next comparison, controls and sample records I would want before interpreting a new experiment.

How it works

There are 2,610 deposited measurements across four passage samples. Choose a pair and a short-length threshold; the curves and comparison record are recalculated from the individual rows. A read-length restriction is available as a selection-bias stress test, not as a recommended quality filter. The second analysis runs the authors’ current code on a separate example BAM to make parameter sensitivity visible.

These are four passage samples, not 2,610 independent biological replicates. No clone-level uncertainty or statistical significance is estimated.

Analysis 2 — Make the measurement settings part of the record

Reproduction + sensitivity
One example BAM, three explicit gap settings

I ran Telometer 2.0.3 on the repository’s public example BAM, comparing the CLI’s 20 bp default with the 100 bp example and 250 bp options-table value in the pinned README. Recording each setting makes the comparison reproducible. These are alternatives to inspect, not three biological replicates or a search for the “best” result.

Why this matters for the lab

A follow-up experiment needs both a biological comparison and a stable definition of its measurement. Here the passage analysis asks which readouts deserve to travel together; the code audit asks whether their measurement settings have been recorded explicitly. Together they support a concrete next step: repeat a selected passage comparison with independent clones, matched handling and a complementary length assay, then inspect whether the distribution-level change persists.

This is an inferred opportunity for experimental interpretation and planning, not a claim about the lab’s internal priorities. A new dataset can reuse the comparison after adding sample, clone, genotype, passage, library-batch and read-length fields. Biological variance and independent validation would still be needed before making a mechanistic claim.

Methods note

The primary analysis preserves all 2,610 rows, including 25 identical visible records that cannot safely be deduplicated without original read IDs. Worksheet-row IDs are traceability labels, not molecule IDs. The workbook labels the last passage day 105, whereas the Figure 2 caption says 108; this tool keeps the deposited label. Means, medians and fractions are descriptive, and read restrictions can preferentially remove short telomeres. No p-values, biological confidence intervals or clinical classification are reported.

The separate BAM audit uses Telometer 2.0.3, a pinned repository commit and explicit parameters. A sensitivity flag does not tell us which setting is correct. Source hashes, dependency versions, processing scripts, tests and reuse conditions are in the public repository. Primary provenance · Executed audit and provenance. Analysis and interface prepared with AI assistance; no claim of unaided implementation or experimental validation.
